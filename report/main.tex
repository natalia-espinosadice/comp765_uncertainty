\begin{abstract}
\todo{4--6 sentences. (1) Context: offline model-based RL trains policies inside a learned world
model; \rwmu{} penalizes reward by ensemble disagreement. (2) Gap: the estimate is known to be
overconfident on one robot, but whether that matters for the policy was untested. (3) What we do:
reimplement the estimator on three MuJoCo tasks, ablate the ensemble design, and train 504
policies under different penalties. (4) Main result: ranks errors, too small; policies exploit
errors all members share; strong penalties reward early termination; an action-support penalty
helps Hopper but not HalfCheetah. (5) Takeaway.}
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Hypotheses.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{H1} The shared-trunk ensemble design causes the overconfidence (independent trunks,
        bootstrapping, more members should fix it).
  \item \textbf{H2} Uncertainty tracks error mostly because both grow with horizon.
  \item \textbf{H3} Uncertainty detects shifted data less well than the true error would.
  \item \textbf{H4} The choice and scale of the uncertainty penalty determine whether a policy
        trained in imagination transfers to the real environment.
\end{itemize}
